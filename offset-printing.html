Offset printing remains the industry standard for high-volume commercial print jobs. By transferring ink from a plate to a rubber blanket, and then to the printing surface, it delivers consistently sharp, clean, and vibrant images.

The Economics of Offset Printing

With offset printing, the bulk of the cost is in the initial setup—creating the metal plates and calibrating the press. Once the press is running, the cost per unit drops dramatically. This makes offset printing incredibly economical for large quantities.

We recommend offset printing for any job exceeding 1,000 units. Not only do you benefit from economies of scale, but you also gain access to true Pantone spot colors, metallic inks, and specialty paper stocks that are not compatible with digital presses.

How We Work

Our Offset Process

1

Pre-Press & Proofing

We review your files, generate CTP (Computer-to-Plate) files, and provide a hard-copy proof for color and layout approval.

2

Plate Making

Custom aluminum plates are etched for each color channel (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Black, and any spot colors).

3

Printing

The plates are loaded onto the press. We calibrate ink levels to match your exact brand colors before running the full job.

4

Finishing & Delivery

Sheets are allowed to dry, then cut, folded, bound, or die-cut as required before being securely packed and delivered.

FAQ

Common Questions

Why is offset printing more expensive for small runs?

Offset printing requires the creation of physical metal plates and extensive press setup before printing can begin. These fixed costs make it uneconomical for small quantities compared to digital printing.

What is Pantone (PMS) color matching?

The Pantone Matching System uses pre-mixed inks to ensure an exact, standardized color every time. Offset printing allows us to use these specific inks to match your brand colors perfectly.

How long does an offset print job take?

Due to the setup, plate-making, printing, drying, and finishing processes, standard offset jobs typically take 3 to 7 working days, depending on complexity.

Can you print on textured or specialty paper?

Yes, offset printing is highly versatile and can print on a much wider variety of paper weights, textures, and finishes than digital presses.
