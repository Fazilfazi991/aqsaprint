Limitless Printing Possibilities

Ultraviolet (UV) printing represents the next generation of commercial printing. Unlike conventional inks that dry through evaporation, UV inks are instantly cured (dried) using high-intensity ultraviolet lights.

This instant curing process means the ink doesn't have time to soak into the material, allowing us to print incredibly sharp, vibrant images on almost any flat surface, regardless of its texture or porosity.

  • Print directly onto rigid substrates up to 50mm thick
  • Scratch, fade, and water-resistant finish out of the machine
  • White ink capability for printing on dark or transparent materials
  • Clear varnish for spot UV, embossed, and textured effects

White Ink & Textured Varnish

One of the most powerful features of our flatbed UV printers is the ability to print opaque white ink. This allows us to print vibrant, full-color images on dark, colored, or clear materials without the colors looking washed out.

We can also apply layers of clear UV varnish. This can be used for "Spot UV" effects to highlight specific areas of a design, or layered repeatedly to build up actual physical textures and embossed effects that you can feel.

FAQ

Common Questions

Can you print on uneven or curved surfaces?

Flatbed UV printing requires a mostly flat surface. We can accommodate minor textures, but the printhead must pass very close to the surface, making it unsuitable for highly curved objects.

Is UV printing safe for outdoors?

Yes. UV cured inks are inherently highly resistant to fading from sunlight and are waterproof, making them excellent for long-term outdoor signage.

Can I provide my own materials?

Yes, we frequently print on client-supplied materials (e.g., custom wood panels, specific metal enclosures). We will run a quick test patch first to ensure ink adhesion.

How durable is the print on glass or acrylic?

Very durable. The UV curing process bonds the ink tightly to the substrate. For glass and acrylic, we often print on the reverse side (sub-surface) so the material itself protects the print.
